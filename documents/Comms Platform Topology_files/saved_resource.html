Comms Platform Topology

Seven Spring Boot applications, four backing stores and one observability stack. This is what talks to what, and on which port — drawn as two planes, because every service touches config, discovery and telemetry, and drawing those edges on the request path turns it into a hairball.

Request plane — how a notification travels

One path, left to right, with the delivery outcome looping back to the store it started in. Rounded boxes are Spring applications; cylinders and the hexagon are infrastructure; dashed boxes sit outside the platform.

Platform plane — what every service depends on

These edges exist for all seven applications. Note the direction change at the bottom: traces and logs are pushed to the collector, metrics are pulled from it. That asymmetry is why a service that dies stops appearing in Tempo but still registers as down in Prometheus.

Port map

Every service runs on the same host locally, so each needs its own pair. A service and its actuator share their last two digits — 8040 pairs with 9040 — and services sit five apart, in startup order.

Component Tier Service Actuator Role
upstreamSimulator edge 8035 9035 Stands in for the external system that raises notifications
cloudGateway edge 8030 9030 Single entry point; routes by service id from the registry
ingestionService core 8040 9040 Accepts and stores the request, owns the transactional outbox
dispatchService core 8045 9045 Consumes by priority, retries, dead-letters, reports the outcome
providerService core 8050 9050 Talks to the email and SMS gateways
eurekaServer platform 8025 9025 Service registry
configServer platform 8020 9020 Serves configuration from config-repo-native
MongoDB data 27017 — Single-node replica set rs0, for multi-document transactions
PostgreSQL data 5432 — Dead-letter store
Redis data 6379 — Idempotency keys, 24h locally / 72h deployed
Kafka data 9092 — Five topics; partitions weighted 4/3/2/1 by priority
Buggregator edge 8000 · 1025 — Captures the email and SMS that would have been sent
Grafana LGTM platform 3000 · 4318 — Tempo, Loki, Prometheus and Grafana in one container

The gaps are deliberate. Five between each service leaves room to slot a new one in without renumbering its neighbours, and the whole block sits clear of the ports the containers publish — Kafka on 9092, Grafana on 3000, Buggregator on 8000. The numbers ascend in startup order — configuration 8020, registry 8025, edge 8030/8035, then the pipeline in the direction a notification travels — while the table below groups by tier.

Design decisions worth knowing

The outbox, not a direct publish

ingestionService writes the notification and its outbox row in one Mongo transaction, then a scheduled relay publishes to Kafka. A crash between the two leaves work to retry rather than a notification nobody sent.

Priority as separate topics

Four topics rather than one with a priority field, so a flood of low-priority traffic cannot delay a critical send. Partitions are the unit of consumer parallelism, so they are weighted to match concurrency — 4/3/2/1 — and scale together via one unit setting.

Retry lives in the service

Delivery is retried on the consumer thread, which holds the partition and preserves ordering. Binder-level retry is off — two uncoordinated retry loops around one call is worse than one.

Trace context on the outbox row

The relay runs on a scheduler thread, long after the HTTP request ended. The W3C traceparent is stored on the row and re-opened at publish time, so one trace spans the gateway, both hops and the provider call.

Actuator on its own connector

Management endpoints are off the port the gateway routes to. Scrape traffic is also excluded from observations, so Tempo and the RED panels describe real requests rather than the monitoring system.

Config from a server, not the jar

Six services import their configuration from configServer at startup. configServer and eurekaServer cannot be clients of themselves, so they carry their own copy of the shared observability block.

Ports and topology verified against the running stack. Service ports are overridable with SERVER_PORT, management ports with MANAGEMENT_SERVER_PORT.
